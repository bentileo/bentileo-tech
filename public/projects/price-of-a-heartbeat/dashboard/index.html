Sources and how these numbers are built
  • Prices. Hospital price-transparency files compiled in the Oria lake (Trilliant Health, snapshot ): every US hospital file listing these codes. Every contract row that publishes a dollar amount is used, whatever its contract type (fee schedule, percent of charges, case rate, per diem); fee-schedule rows alone miss percent-of-charge contracts, which are the largest group and price about twice as high. Each contract is classified from its payer and plan names and only commercial contracts are priced; Medicare Advantage, Medicaid and government contracts are left out, as are inpatient rows. Four cleaning rules remove rows that are not real negotiated facility rates: physician fees published inside facility files (under 50% of Medicare’s rate), “negotiated” amounts at or above the hospital’s own gross charge, amounts above six times Medicare when no gross charge is published, and any hospital file that prices a lesser study above the complete one (single vs. multi-study nuclear, imaging-only vs. complete stress echo, echo without vs. with Doppler). A state’s price is the median across its hospital files, each file’s rate being its median across commercial contracts; the panel says how many files sit behind each figure. A state with fewer than three usable files for a service takes its census-division median and is marked estimated. The resulting national medians match independent published studies of the same files (JASE 2023 and JAHA 2025 report $1,313–$1,364 for a resting echo and $1,165 for a stress echo; here $1,392 and $1,235).
  • Medicare benchmark is the national OPPS payment for 2026 (October update), not adjusted for local wages. All cardiac rehab codes pay $131.70 per session.
  • Patients (cardiac rehab only). CDC Interactive Atlas of Heart Disease and Stroke, Medicare fee-for-service, 2019–2020. Eligible counts beneficiaries with a qualifying event per 1,000; start is the share of eligible patients with at least one session within a year; finish is the share of starters reaching 36 sessions. County figures only, so a state is its typical (median) county. No public source reports referrals, and none reports volumes for stress tests.
  • Hospitals offering cardiac rehab come from the CDC’s 2022 hospital service map.
  • Your clinic. Without your data, cardiac rehab shows a per-100-eligible-patients benchmark for the chosen state; stress tests have no public volume benchmark. With your monthly data, volumes are fitted with a linear trend plus month-of-year seasonality (24+ months), checked on the last 6 months held out, and projected 12 months with an 80% range. Revenue is volume × a blended rate (your Medicare share at Medicare’s rate, the rest at the state’s commercial rate for that service), so it ignores denials and copays.
  • One price snapshot and one patient period, so no national trend yet. A published rate is a contract term, not proof of what was paid. Map geometry: US Census cartographic boundaries (Albers, via us-atlas).

Disclosure. Built by a physiologist and data scientist from public data only; no hospital or payer data beyond the published files.
