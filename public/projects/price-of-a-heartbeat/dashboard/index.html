How these numbers are built
  • Prices. Hospital price-transparency files compiled in the Oria lake (Trilliant Health, snapshot ). Only fee-schedule contract rates from commercial plans are used; Medicare Advantage, Medicaid and traditional Medicare contracts are classified from plan names and left out. Rows labeled as professional fees are excluded, and so are rates under 25% of Medicare, which are almost always physician fees or unit errors. A state’s price is the median across its price files, each file’s rate being its median across commercial contracts; states with fewer than 10 files are left blank. Some states’ commercial rates come out unusually low (Texas is about $79 a session), which more likely reflects how their hospitals file rates than what plans actually pay.
  • Patients. CDC Interactive Atlas of Heart Disease and Stroke, Medicare fee-for-service, 2019–2020. Participation is the share of eligible patients with at least one session within a year of their event; completion is the share of starters reaching 36 sessions. The CDC publishes these by county only, so a state’s value here is its typical (median) county, not a patient-weighted average. Small counties are suppressed by the CDC.
  • Unbilled Medicare sessions per 100 eligible patients compare today’s participation × sessions with the Million Hearts goal of 70% participation and a full 36-session program, valued at Medicare’s 2026 rate of $131.70 per session.
  • Hospitals with cardiac rehab are CDC’s 2022 map layer (2,210 sites, 22 not assignable to a state).
  • Washington detail. “Typical rate” is the median of a hospital’s fee-schedule rates across its insurer contracts. Peers are hospitals of the same CMS type; each price file counts once, however many campuses it covers. Insurer comparisons need at least hospitals with that insurer.
  • Your program. Without your data, figures are per 100 eligible Medicare patients in the chosen state’s typical county, using CDC participation and sessions and that state’s commercial price; US top quarter means the 75th-percentile county (47.5% start, 27 sessions). With your monthly data, sessions are fitted with a linear trend plus month-of-year seasonality (24+ months), checked on the last 6 months held out, and projected 12 months with an 80% range. Levers scale referrals × enrollment × sessions per patient, phased in over 6 months.
  • Medicare is the national OPPS payment for 2026 (October update), not adjusted for local wages. A published rate is a contract term, not proof of what was paid.
